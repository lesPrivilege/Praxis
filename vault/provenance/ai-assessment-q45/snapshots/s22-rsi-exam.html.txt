  <h1><span class="nm">RSI-Exam</span>: Benchmarking Recursive
    <span class="nb">Self-Improvement</span> through Executable Research</h1>
  <p class="sub">It evaluates whether an AI agent can improve itself over a long horizon and
    generalize to unseen data. Hours of autonomous experimentation on the method that solves the task or
    the harness that drives a frozen model, then one final run on a hidden test set.</p>

<section id="overview"><div class="wrap">
  <span class="eyebrow e-blue">Overview</span>
  <h2>The evaluation pipeline</h2>
  <p class="lead">In the working container an agent receives the task instruction and the autoresearch
    protocol. It then improves its approach round after round, guided by feedback on the visible data.
    When it is done, the artifact it produced is handed to the verifier container and scored on hidden
    data.</p>

  <div class="fig"><svg class="pfig" viewBox="0 0 1200 592" role="img"
 aria-label="Left: the agent image on visible data. The agent is handed two things of comparable weight - the task instruction, which fixes the interface, the data, the metric and the rules, and the autoresearch protocol, whose three standing rules are to loop forever, to keep every version in the trail, and to aim at what transfers because the visible score is only a proxy. Under those two the agent runs a loop: edit the weak baseline, self-check on visible data, repeat for many rounds. Only the artifact crosses the one-way seal. Right: the verifier image, sealed and offline, reruns the artifact in a fresh container on hidden data, reads the task's own raw metric, and places it on the anchor scale to give one normalised score.">
