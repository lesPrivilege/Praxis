Treat this as a subtraction-first audit.

Look specifically for:

- instructions added to compensate for limitations of older models that Astra no longer needs;
- unconditional document-reading, planning, testing, review, or approval rituals;
- duplicated or contradictory instructions across AGENTS.md, skills, prompts, and agent profiles;
- oversized skill descriptions or skills that should instead be thin routers with progressive disclosure;
- overly broad skill triggers that inject irrelevant context;
- step-by-step recipes that unnecessarily constrain model judgment;
- defensive permission boundaries that cause Astra to stop when safe, reversible work could continue;
- completion rules that encourage premature handoff or repeated user prompting;
- unnecessary verification or testing loops;
- model-specific guidance that has accidentally become a universal rule;
- stale routing assumptions now that Astra, Sol, and Luna have different useful roles;
- opportunities for bounded parallel exploration or cheap helper delegation without imposing a fixed agent topology.

Do not optimize for a smaller prompt merely for its own sake. Preserve constraints that encode real product intent, safety boundaries, architectural invariants, user preferences, or hard-won failure knowledge.

For every proposed removal or rewrite, require a concrete reason:

1. what the current instruction causes;
2. why that behavior is now harmful, redundant, or too broad;
3. what capability or narrower instruction replaces it;
4. what could regress if it is removed.

Do not invent problems in order to justify a cleanup. Prefer leaving a sound instruction unchanged over speculative simplification.

Where the evidence is strong and the change is local and reversible, make the minimal edit directly. Preserve useful history in git rather than keeping obsolete instructions active as comments. For ambiguous architectural or policy decisions, record the finding and recommendation instead of silently changing semantics.

Keep model routing as capability priors rather than mandatory choreography. Astra may adjudicate or handle unusually difficult work; Sol and Luna may handle suitable execution or exploration. Choose delegation dynamically based on the task instead of satisfying a predefined topology.

At the end, leave the workspace in a state where a fresh capable agent can:

1. discover what matters without loading the whole repository;
2. find the durable output of this ChatGPT session;
3. follow provenance back to important external sources;
4. understand the small set of real operating constraints;
5. perform ordinary work without fighting legacy harness scaffolding.
