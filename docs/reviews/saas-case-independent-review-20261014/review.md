# Independent review of the six SaaS case packages

The 33 cases form a usable teaching baseline. The sampled worked calculations and consequential source assertions held up, and the chapters generally distinguish standards, provider behavior and synthetic design well. I found **eight low-severity issues**, chiefly helper input/conflict handling, test-coverage clarity and unfinished references. No high- or medium-severity defect was confirmed under the examples' stated valid assumptions. This is a scoped review result, not a claim that every fact or business invariant has been proved.

## Evidence identity, terminology and transfer

The case coverage contains **101 source-check records spanning 93 distinct URLs**, including partial, inaccessible and not-reopened entries. Those counts must not be read as 93 fully verified sources. The coordinator's follow-ups and resolutions are separately recorded in [source-review.json](source-review.json). The consolidated source inventory has 149 URLs, including three editorial-method sources; no exhaustive re-audit of all 164 source-fact claims is claimed.

The strongest aspect of these chapters is their separation of evidence roles. Hash lookup, half-open intervals, DAG/topological ordering, largest-remainder allocation, transactions and dependency traversal are established techniques. FHIR, DICOM, SCIM, FIX and EPCIS are standards with versioned scope. Stripe invoice/payment behavior, Okta provisioning choices, Greenhouse Offer fields, Snowflake stream/lineage behavior and vendor workflow statuses are provider-specific facts. Local state names, authority assignments, thresholds, budgets, technology-stack choices and the cross-case map are editorial or synthetic designs. No invented industry consensus or generic algorithm falsely attributed to provider internals was confirmed in the sampled comparisons. This is positive evidence about those comparisons, not a census of every sentence.

All 42 selected transfer entries explicitly use `basis: inference`; they preserve a mechanism while identifying changed conditions, counterexamples and observable checks. I read all entries for those distinctions and sampled their chapter context: H01→V04→V20, H07→V03→V09, V06→V16, V20→V19 and transport capacity, V14→V04, X02→V04, H08→X01, and V17→H04. None of those sampled transfers licenses a clinical decision, quality release, financial payment or account action merely from graph reachability or another domain's completed status. The other author proposals were structurally retained, not all substantively re-evaluated.

Selected follow-ups removed two review uncertainties. The Kingdee EAS 8.6 article supports its narrowly described physical/virtual document workflow and configuration-dependent reversals; it does not establish the synthetic consolidation algorithm. Okta's SCIM 2.0 guide explicitly confirms that suspended users do not generate a deprovisioning event, distinct from deactivation/removal. See the [Kingdee workflow](https://vip.kingdee.com/eascloud/help/8.6/SCM.web/topics/noname1184.html) and [Okta Delete users section](https://developer.okta.com/docs/api/openapi/okta-scim/guides/scim-20#delete-users).

Some gaps remain honestly unclosed. Stripe's idempotency API page returned only a thin documentation landing excerpt in this review; its detailed retention/cache assertions were not newly verified. The FIX 4.4 AllocationInstruction redirect could not be retrieved, while SettlementInstructions could be read and supports an instruction/settlement distinction. The original DICOM overview access gap is not a substitute for checking the specific standard: the separately reopened PS3.18 supports the scoped studies/series/instances and conformance-statement claims. Living URLs remain unsnapshotted dependencies of this review; no new full-source snapshots were created. Per-source limitations remain in the coverage record rather than being silently converted to passes.

## Teaching and handoff judgment

Each case gives the intended reader a way to recognize a problem, choose a method, work numbers, test a failure and change a premise. The full explanations and counterexamples are useful and should survive editing. Local repairs should strengthen the transitions between those steps, especially the distinction between a checked arithmetic helper and a future end-to-end behavior definition.

Several tempting findings were rejected. X01's quota helper alone permits reuse of a settled run ID, but it is expressly a serial accounting model; an outer run lifecycle is needed and the capacity invariant is not violated by that probe. H08's authorization helper is explicitly a pure guard, with atomic claiming assigned to the durable transaction. V03 explicitly says production inbox/quarantine logging remains unimplemented. These limitations should stay visible in evidence and implementation guidance; they should not be mislabeled as provider failures.

New facts, changed semantics or implementation claims require new checking. Keep audit receipts, model provenance and repair tracking in this review directory, outside textbook prose.

No live provider requests, browser end-to-end flows, production database isolation tests, load tests, real AI evaluations, formal regulatory determinations or learner studies were performed. No merge, CI enablement or deployment was requested or performed.
